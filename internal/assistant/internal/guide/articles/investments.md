A brokerage account holds a cash account and one account per **Security**. A trade buys or sells a security for cash of the same brokerage; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis** at **Average cost**. **Investment income**, such as dividends, is paid into the brokerage's cash and may have tax withheld.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest stored **Price** of its **Instrument**. Instruments and prices are shared by every book of the instance. Fixed income securities can carry terms from which equori accrues a daily price.

The Portfolio screen adds the positions and the brokerage cash into one value, with the gain over cost, the income of the year and the split by type, currency or institution. Every asset account and **Net worth** are on the Reports screen.

The return section of the Portfolio screen measures a brokerage, a security, a savings account or a reserve over a period of at most 366 days. It shows the annual money-weighted return and the time-weighted return of the period. Where the book's region has a daily reference rate and a monthly inflation index, the figures compare with them; in Brazil these are the CDI and the IPCA. A region without them, or a report valued in another currency than the index, shows the period return alone. The yield must be categorized under Yield or a sub-category of it, not recorded as a transfer. Any other income, expense or transfer in the account counts as money put in or taken out. The figures depend on what the account holds. A savings account, a reserve or fixed income in the report's currency pays a rate: it shows its rate a year, on the year basis of the reference rate (in Brazil, 252 business days for the CDI), and its percent of the reference rate. An account with stocks, funds, crypto or a foreign currency has a market price: it shows the return of the days compared, not annualized, beside the reference rate over the same days and the difference in percentage points. The real return deducts the inflation index over the whole months with a published rate. The days an account held nothing are left out of the comparison. A savings account is compared from its first recorded yield when none was recorded before the period. A bank that reports the balance net of the taxes it provisions shows a percent below the contracted gross rate. On the Accounts screen, the groups that hold such accounts add a Yield column over 90 days, with the days compared. A rate a year shows from 21 days, a percent shows its margin (±X pp) when large, and a missing figure states why. The page of such an account shows a Yield over the last 12 months section: the comparison with the reference rate and the return net of inflation, each with its dates and figures, and the income and expense treated as money in or out, not yield. Checking accounts carry no yield, because their income is mostly salary.
