A brokerage account holds a cash account and one account per **Security**. A trade buys or sells a security for cash of the same brokerage; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis** at **Average cost**. **Investment income**, such as dividends, is paid into the brokerage's cash and may have tax withheld.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest stored **Price** of its **Instrument**. Every book of the instance shares instruments and prices. A fixed income security can carry terms that accrue a daily price. Defining an instrument searches the enabled sources and shows their quote. Its page shows, loads and imports quotes and sets its symbol at a source.

The Portfolio screen adds the positions and the brokerage cash into one value, with the gain over cost, the income of the year and the split by type, currency or institution. The Reports screen holds every asset account and **Net worth**.

The return section of the Portfolio screen measures a brokerage, a security, a savings account or a reserve over a period of at most 366 days. One sentence states the result first. Two comparisons follow, each a pair of bars on one axis with a chip: against the daily reference rate and against the monthly inflation index (the real return, net of the index over the whole months with a published rate). In Brazil these are the CDI and the IPCA. A region without them, or a report in another currency than the index, shows only the period return. The In money panel shows the opening balance, the net deposits (when there were flows), the gain and the closing balance; on the Portfolio screen a bar splits the growth into deposits and gain. The closed Details and method block holds the rate a year on the year basis of the reference rate (in Brazil, 252 business days), the percent of the reference rate, the yield credits, the time-weighted return and the flows. The yield must be categorized under Yield or a sub-category, not a transfer. Any other income, expense or transfer in the account is money in or out. A savings account, a reserve or fixed income in the report's currency pays a rate and shows its percent of the reference rate. An account with stocks, funds, crypto or a foreign currency has a market price and shows the return of the days compared, not annualized. Days with nothing held are left out. A savings account with no yield recorded before the period is compared from its first one. A bank that reports the balance net of provisioned taxes shows a percent below the gross rate. On the Accounts screen, the groups that hold such accounts add a Yield column over 90 days, with the days compared. A rate a year shows from 21 days, a percent shows its margin (±X pp) when large, and a missing figure states why. The page of such an account has a Yield tab beside Movements, whose Yield over the last 12 months section draws the same sentence, comparisons, panel and details. Checking accounts carry no yield: their income is mostly salary.
