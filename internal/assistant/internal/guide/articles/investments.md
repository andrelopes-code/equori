A brokerage account holds a cash account and one account per **Security**. A trade buys or sells a security for cash of the same brokerage; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis** at **Average cost**. **Investment income**, such as dividends, is paid into the brokerage's cash and may have tax withheld.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest stored **Price** of its **Instrument**. Instruments and prices are shared by every book of the instance. Fixed income securities can carry terms from which equori accrues a daily price.

Positions show on the Account screen of a brokerage; allocation, return and **Net worth** come from the reports.

The return section of the Portfolio screen measures a brokerage, a security, a savings account or a reserve over a period of at most 366 days. It shows the annual money-weighted return, the time-weighted return of the period, and, where the region has a reference index (CDI in Brazil), the percent of that index that grows as much over the same days. A savings account sold as "100% of CDI" reads close to 100% when it paid what it promised. The yield must be recorded as income, not as a transfer: a transfer counts as money put in. On the Accounts screen, the groups that hold a savings account, a reserve or a brokerage add a Yield (90 days) column: the rate a year on the basis the index is quoted on (252 business days for CDI) and the percent of the index. An account opened inside the window is compared from the day after its first deposit. The page of such an account shows its yield over 12 months, with the percent of the index and the index's own rate a year. Checking accounts carry no yield, because their income is mostly salary.
