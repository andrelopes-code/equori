A brokerage account holds a cash account and one account per **Security**. A trade buys or sells a security for cash of the same brokerage; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis** at **Average cost**. **Investment income**, such as dividends, is paid into the brokerage's cash and may have tax withheld.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest stored **Price** of its **Instrument**. Instruments and prices are shared by every book of the instance. Fixed income securities can carry terms from which equori accrues a daily price.

Positions show on the Account screen of a brokerage. The Portfolio screen adds the positions and the brokerage cash into one value, states the gain over cost and the income of the year, splits the value by type, currency or institution, and offers to record a trade or an income. The allocation of every asset account and **Net worth** are on the Reports screen.

The return section of the Portfolio screen measures a brokerage, a security, a savings account or a reserve over a period of at most 366 days. It shows the annual money-weighted return and the time-weighted return of the period. Where the book's region has a daily reference rate and a monthly inflation index, the figures compare with them; in Brazil these are the CDI and the IPCA, and a region without them shows the period return alone. The comparison applies only to a report valued in the currency of the index. The yield must be recorded as income, not as a transfer: a transfer counts as money put in. The figures depend on what the account holds. A savings account, a reserve or fixed income in the report's currency pays a rate: it shows its rate a year, on the year basis the reference rate is quoted on (252 business days for the CDI), and its percent of the reference rate. In Brazil, a savings account sold as "100% of CDI" reads close to 100% when it paid what it promised. An account with stocks, funds, crypto or a foreign currency has a market price: it shows the return of the days compared, not annualized, beside the reference rate over the same days and the difference in percentage points, because a percent of a reference rate says nothing about a market price. The real return deducts the inflation index over the whole months with a published rate. The days an account held nothing are left out of the comparison. A savings account is compared from its first recorded yield when none was recorded before the period: days before it would read as days that paid nothing. The yield is what the account records; a bank that reports the balance net of the income tax it provisions shows a percent below the contracted gross rate. On the Accounts screen, the groups that hold such accounts add a Yield (90 days) column. The page of such an account shows a Yield over the last 12 months section: the comparison with the reference rate and the return net of inflation, each with its own dates, its key figures as cards and the figures they come from below. Checking accounts carry no yield, because their income is mostly salary.
