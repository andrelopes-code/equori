A brokerage account holds a cash account and one account per **Security**. A trade buys or sells a security for cash of the same brokerage; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis** at **Average cost**. **Investment income**, such as dividends, is paid into the brokerage's cash and may have tax withheld.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest stored **Price** of its **Instrument**. Instruments and prices are shared by every book of the instance. Fixed income securities can carry terms from which equori accrues a daily price.

Positions show on the Account screen of a brokerage; allocation, return and **Net worth** come from the reports.

The return section of the Portfolio screen measures a brokerage, a security, a savings account or a reserve over a period of at most 366 days. It shows the annual money-weighted return, the time-weighted return of the period, and, where the region has a reference index (CDI in Brazil), the percent of that index that grows as much over the same days. A savings account sold as "100% of CDI" reads close to 100% when it paid what it promised. The yield must be recorded as income, not as a transfer: a transfer counts as money put in. The figures depend on what the account holds. A savings account, a reserve or fixed income in reais pays a rate: it shows its rate a year, on the 252 business days the CDI is quoted on, and its percent of the CDI. An account with stocks, funds, crypto or a foreign currency has a market price: it shows the return of the days compared, not annualized, beside the CDI over the same days and the difference in percentage points, because a percent of the CDI says nothing about a market price. The real return deducts the IPCA over the whole months with a published rate. The days an account held nothing are left out of the comparison. A savings account is compared from its first recorded yield when none was recorded before the period: days before it would read as days that paid nothing. The yield is what the account records; a bank that reports the balance net of the income tax it provisions shows a percent below the contracted gross rate. On the Accounts screen, the groups that hold such accounts add a Yield (90 days) column. The page of such an account shows a Yield over the last 12 months section: the comparison with the CDI and the return net of the IPCA, each with its own dates, its key figures as cards and the figures they come from below. Checking accounts carry no yield, because their income is mostly salary.
