equori keeps a double-entry **Ledger** of your money. Every **Account** you own (checking, cash, card, brokerage) and every **Category** of income or expense is an account of the ledger. A **Transaction** moves money between accounts through its **Posting** lines, which always add up to zero.

Start with your accounts and their opening balances on the Accounts screen, then record transactions by hand, by text capture, by importing bank files or by linking a bank. The Overview screen summarizes the month; Transactions lists everything by day.

The assistant reads your data, explains concepts and screens, and prepares changes. Every change it prepares is a proposal you confirm or reject on a card in the conversation; nothing changes before you confirm.

On a wide screen, a destination of the navigation column whose page has tabs (Transactions, Categories, Accounts, Planning, Reports) is one row with an arrow at its end: a press on its name or icon goes to the page and opens its tabs as rows under the destination, each one a direct link to that tab; a press on the arrow only opens or closes the tabs, without leaving the current page, and the browser keeps the groups left open. With the column narrowed to icons, the row has no arrow: its press goes to the page and lists the tabs beside the icon.

On a wide screen, the key C or the Calculator command of the navigation column opens a calculator that floats over any screen. Type the whole account on one line (for example `1200 * 1,05 - 300`); the result updates on every key, Enter keeps it as the start of the next account, and the copy button puts it on the clipboard. Drag the window by its bar; Escape closes it, and the account stays on it until you clear it.

The Help screen opens with a search: it reads the titles, the sections, the glossary terms and the text of the help pages, ignores accents and case, and accepts one changed letter. The key / focuses the search from any help page; the arrow keys move through the results, Enter opens one and Escape clears. Under the search, Help lists the first-month walkthrough as numbered stops, the pages by area of the application (Accounts, Cards, Transactions, Portfolio, Planning, Installation), the symptoms of the troubleshooting page and the glossary terms. A help page lists its sections beside the text and numbers its steps; a page with figures shows the screen with the place of each step marked, and may offer a short video of the steps, without sound. The pages with figures are Import a statement, Pay a statement, Check an account's balance, Create a category rule, Record a transaction, Record transactions as text, Correct or void a transaction, Record a reimbursement, Create a schedule, Create a budget, Buy an investment, Record a purchase in installments and Connect a bank; each of them but Connect a bank offers a video. The reference pages The statement screen, The portfolio, The contribution plan and The month's plan carry figures without a video. The video opens in a player over the page, with the steps listed beside it; its controls play and pause, move along the video and open it in full screen; a press on the video or the space bar plays or pauses it and the arrow keys go to the previous or next step. Under reduced motion each step shows still, without the pointer's travel. The player closes with its button, with Escape or with a press outside it, and the focus returns to the button "Watch the video". The button in the page header opens the screen the page describes. A page of steps ends with the symptoms of its task, the related pages and a button that opens the assistant with the page title in the message field.

Spreadsheets. A spreadsheet is a table of the member's own, kept in the book beside the accounts: a comparison of two offers, the plan of a trip, a list kept by hand. It has sheets, cells with values and formulas, number formats, conditional formatting, data validation, filters, sorting, notes, links and images. A spreadsheet reads no figure of the accounts and changes none: what a cell holds is what was typed or pasted into it.

The Spreadsheets screen lists the spreadsheets of the book, the last saved first, and searches them by name. "New spreadsheet" creates one and opens it. The menu of a row holds "Open", "Rename", "Make a copy" and "Delete". A deletion is not undone. A book holds 40 spreadsheets.

An open spreadsheet saves by itself after each pause in the changes and on Ctrl+S. The header states "Saved", "Unsaved changes", "Saving" or "Not saved". When the same spreadsheet was saved from another window, the screen stops saving and offers two ways out: "Open the saved version" replaces the screen with what is stored, and "Keep this screen as a copy" stores the screen as a new spreadsheet. A member who can only read opens a spreadsheet as "View only".

The spreadsheets are part of the export of the book and of the backups.

The assistant does not read, create or change spreadsheets. It points to the Spreadsheets screen.
