A Brokerage holds one Investment line per security. Its money is in a Cash account inside it or in an account of its institution, chosen on the New account screen. A purchase takes two steps: create a brokerage, then record a buy. The first buy of a security creates its Investment line; none is created by hand. A trade buys or sells for the Cash or an open account of the institution; the **Position** keeps the quantity and the **Cost basis**; a sale removes cost at **Average cost**, or oldest purchase first for fixed income in Brazil. get_holding_lots reads this for one Investment line: the method (fifo or average), quantity, cost basis and, under fifo, each open purchase with date, quantity and cost at the end of a date. **Investment income** (dividends) is paid into the Cash or that account and may have tax withheld.

A Brokerage with a holding that keeps a Cash account with no entry beside one open account of its institution asks which account pays each trade; record_investment without cash_account_id returns brokerage_cash_ambiguous. The page of the Brokerage then shows a notice whose action closes that Cash account on today's date, as close_account does; trades and income then use the account of the institution without a choice. Reopening the Cash account undoes it.

The **Market value** of a position uses the latest **Price** of its **Instrument**. Fixed income terms accrue a daily price from the latest manual or official price. Defining an instrument shows the quote of enabled sources. probe_quote does the same before manage_instrument define: with code, type, quote_currency and, when needed, exchange and symbols, it returns the latest quote of each enabled source that covers the security (or its error) and the disabled sources; it stores nothing and needs the write role. record_price_series records several dated prices of one instrument, all or nothing, with code, quote_instrument and prices; a typed price for a date is replaced, and a date a source covers keeps the price of the source (below_feed). import_price_file records the prices of a csv file attached to the conversation, as the price import of Instruments does: file_id is the id of the [file ...] line of the message; the header is instrument,quote_instrument,date,value, at most 10,000 lines, all or nothing, and the card shows the file name and the line count. Its page opens with the latest price and its change, a chart of the range with manual prices and the average cost (drag to see a period's change), the position with its unrealized result, and a fixed income term with liquidity and grace date; a listed real estate fund with a stored report shows the fund report: the figures filed for its latest month, the price over book value, the twelve-month distributions over the price and the break-even growth against the longest bond of the real curve; when two funds of the source report the same code, the page lists both with their segment and identifier and Link stores the chosen one as the symbol of the source (get_market_data view fund_reports returns them as fund_report_candidates; manage_instrument links one by symbols); a stock whose company files with the regulator shows the company statements: price over earnings and over book value, with their range over the fiscal years, when a second filing confirms the count of shares; the profit and the revenue against the same months of the year before; return on equity and net margin over twelve months, net debt over equity, the years with profit among the five latest and the nine Piotroski signals as facts, without a sum (none for a financial company, which the studies leave out); equori gives no recommendation on a fund report or a company statement; the prices by date, one year at a time, and the sources with dates open in layers; a table of price changes by month and year; it ends with links to other sites. It loads and imports quotes, sets its symbol at a source, retypes, hides and (when unused) removes it. The list shows the price date and the changes over a week, month, quarter, year and 12 months. Columns, beside its search, hides, orders and pins the columns; the choice stays in that browser.

The Portfolio screen adds positions and Cash into one value, with the gain over cost, the total result (that gain plus every sale and every income net of tax since the first purchase, closed positions included), the income of the year, the split by type, currency or institution and a 3-month price line per position; the list adds the total result of each position, which a part in another currency leaves empty. The screen has five tabs: Results holds those figures at today's date, then the return against the indexes, the rolling windows, the months, the value of the portfolio and the result of each holding; Positions holds the split, fixed income and the list of holdings; Income and tax holds the realized results, the income and the tax of a year; Contributions holds the contribution plan; Projection holds the plan to the withdrawal age, described below. get_portfolio view positions gives each position its realized results and net income. Cash goes below zero when a purchase was recorded without the transfer of money to the brokerage: its row in the split shows a dash, the other shares add up to 100% and recording the transfer corrects it. A notice names holdings with a late or no quote. Reports holds every asset account and **Net worth**. get_portfolio view net_worth reads assets, liabilities and their difference on a date; view net_worth_days reads the same at the end of each day from from to to, at most 62 days.

The Projection tab works by age; the birth date is set in Settings. Plan takes the ages, real return, inflation and a withdrawal (income a month or rate a year); optional: cautious and favorable returns, return during withdrawals, tax on gain. Save plan stores it. Withdrawals may have started already. Wealth journey draws the value, the amount needed and the income sustained. Scenarios compares the three returns. Other incomes lists up to 20 (pension, rent) by age; the portfolio pays the rest. Plan against recorded compares the history with the plan. Planned contributions lists up to 40 changes of the monthly amount. Year by year lists each year.

During a trading session the portfolio values a holding at the current quote its source reports, with the hour of the quote beside the value, and marks the value as partial: it changes until the close. The history, the days before today and every recorded transaction use each day's close, never a partial value. A holding whose source reports no current quote (fixed income, a fund, Tesouro Direto) keeps its last close.

The return block of the Results tab measures All brokerages (savings, reserves and checking stay out) or one Brokerage, security, savings account or reserve, over up to 3,660 days. It leads with the gain of the period in money beside the period return, which flows do not change, and the annual rate above a year. Comparisons follow, each over its own window: the daily reference rate on published days, the monthly inflation index over whole published months, with the real return; in Brazil the CDI and the IPCA. Without them, or in another currency, only the period return shows. Where the closing balance came from shows opening balance, net deposits (money in minus out), gain (closing minus opening minus net deposits) and closing balance; a bar splits the closing balance into these parts when the gain is positive and no part is negative. The foot writes the annual rate; Details and method holds the reconciliation, rates, credits, the time-weighted return and monthly flows. Yield is categorized under Yield or a sub-category; other income, expense or transfers are money in or out. Savings, reserves and fixed income in the report's currency show their percent of the reference rate; stocks, funds, crypto or foreign currency show the return of the days compared. On the Accounts screen such groups add a 90-day Yield column; the account page adds a Yield tab with the same blocks.

The calculator "Compare indexers" lists two to six fixed income securities held to one maturity and shows, for each, the gross yield, the tax, the net yield and the net annual rate. In Brazil a taxed security held to a maturity within 30 days pays the IOF of the redemption, which the calculator "Fixed income" lists apart as the redemption tax. The rate of a security tied to an index follows the expected path of that index to the maturity, from a survey of expectations; a fixed rate is typed as it is. Beside the break-even of each security tied to an index, against the reference security (the first fixed rate, or else the first security), come three figures: the expected rate of the index with the date of its survey, the rate of the government bond for the term with the date of its curve, and the inflation implied by government bonds when the index is inflation. A survey over 11 days old and a curve over 6 days old are marked outdated. The break-even is the constant rate of the index at which the two securities pay the same after tax. Rows keep the order typed. The screen gives no recommendation or verdict; the choice is the user's. It does not compare credit risk or liquidity, and a sale before the maturity has another result. The calculator is listed only when an index of the base currency has an expected path.

Results opens on a period bar: 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, This year, 12 months, 2 years, 5 years, From the start, or a range of two dates. The select What to see picks All brokerages or one account; the currency select values the figures in another currency. Return gives the return of the period, how many points it stands above or below each index over the same days, and a list of series: each index is a switch for its line, and the portfolio line stays. Beside it come the annual rate (from 365 days), the return above inflation, the volatility (the deviation of monthly returns, from 12 months) and the largest fall between two dates. Rolling 12-month windows give the return of 12 whole months to each month end over the whole history: the 12 months to today, the best window, the worst, the share below zero and the share above each index. Month by month tabulates the return of each month by year, with the yearly sum and the accumulated return; a select subtracts the return of an index from each month. Evolution of the portfolio draws the value against the money contributed, with opening value, net contributions, return, closing value and the annual rate (IRR) of the period. While the history loads each block shows its labels; a failed read shows a button to read again.

Return by holding, below the return, splits each calendar year among the holdings with one method: every return is the gain over the average money at work in the period, counting the date of each contribution, purchase, sale and income. A year starts on the day there was money at work, and the current year runs to today. A bar chart shows the return of every year with a bar beside it for the same contributions at the CDI; a click on a bar picks the year. A history over twelve years opens on the last twelve; zoom reaches the older ones. The summary of the year draws three bars on one scale (the portfolio, the same money at the CDI, and inflation (IPCA)), each with its return, money and the last day its rates cover; below come the difference in money from the CDI, the real return and how many holdings gained and lost, gains in green and losses in red. Each row holds a holding, a bar of its contribution on an axis centred on zero, its weight (its share of the average money), its return over the days held, its contribution (its gain over the portfolio's average money, in points) and its result. Weights add up to 100% and contributions to the portfolio's return. The five largest and five smallest results show one by one; the rest form one row. A holding whose quote at a year end is over 14 days old writes No quote since and the date, with no bar or return; a year with a holding with no quote names it and shows no figure. Cash, fees and exchange holds the money and result no holding owns. A comparison whose index has a gap over 7 days in the period is left out. A click on its heading, outside the help mark, minimizes it to its first lines or opens it; the browser keeps it. get_portfolio view attribution returns the same years.

The tools. get_portfolio reads the holdings of the book, by view: positions, allocation (by type, currency or institution), net_worth, net_worth_days, return, income_types, subtypes, contribution, fixed_income_events, issuer_exposure and attribution. get_market_data reads what the instance knows of the market, by view: instruments, instrument (one, with its fixed income terms), prices, catalog (the securities the enabled sources can quote, by code or name), calendars, holidays, indexes, index_rates, index_expectations, curves, fund_reports (the monthly reports of a listed fund, by code) company_reports (the accounts a listed company filed, by code) and company_figures (its ratios and price multiples, each with its date or the reason it has none).

record_investment records a trade or an income; kind is buy, sell, opening (held before the book, no cash) or income. The holding is account_id, an Investment line, or brokerage_id with instrument; the first buy of a security at a Brokerage uses the pair. A buy or a sell takes date, quantity, unit_price, and optionally fees and taxes. An income takes date, gross, income_type (from get_portfolio view income_types) and category_id, and optionally withheld, the tax withheld at source. cash_account_id is needed only with several paying accounts. With transaction_id and reason it corrects that trade or income; the call replaces the whole order: repeat every field that stays.

manage_instrument acts on a security; action names the operation and code the security.

- define: name, type (currency, stock, etf, reit, depositary_receipt, fixed_income, fund or crypto), quote_currency and scale, the decimal places of a quantity; exchange is the market code (ISO 10383, such as BVMF), subtype refines the type and symbols gives the symbol at a source when it differs from the code.
- rename: name. edit: type (among stock, etf, reit and depositary_receipt), exchange, subtype, symbols, and the issuer with issuer, issuer_scheme and issuer_id.
- record_price: date and price, one price typed by the user.
- set_terms: the terms of a fixed income security (topic fixed_income).
- request_history: from, the first date; the enabled sources load the prices since then.
- hide removes the security from the lists of this book, show brings it back, and delete removes a security no book uses.

The Instruments screen hides a security with the eye at the start of its row; the checkboxes mark several, and the bar at the foot hides or shows all marked. Show hidden lists them, dimmed and tagged Hidden. A currency cannot be hidden.

Every book of the instance shares instruments and prices; a contract of record_fixed_income belongs to its book alone.

The assistant opens a calculator with its inputs filled through open_calculator, which returns a link to the screen. Amounts are decimal values in the base currency (a value finer than the currency is rejected) and rates are in percent. The screen shows the result, evidence and method; the link carries no figure computed by the assistant.

The calculators list shows each calculator by the question it answers, and only the calculators with a screen. A calculator shows its answer, then the evidence, then the inputs. An optional input, such as a delayed start, a withdrawal or an extra prepayment, stays closed until a button opens it; a button that starts with "Remove" closes it and clears its values. The details and the method stay closed below the inputs. An optional input that the link fills opens by itself.
