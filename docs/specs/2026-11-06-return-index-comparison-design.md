# Return compared with an index

Amends `docs/specs/2026-10-23-investments-design.md` (NG5, section 4.3). Amendment A208.

## Intent

The return report (`GET /reports/return`) gives the money-weighted annual return of an account tree. It does not state whether a savings account sold as "100% of CDI" paid 100% of CDI. This design adds two figures to the report:

- the time-weighted return of the period: the growth of one unit of money held in the tree for the whole period, independent of the size and timing of deposits and withdrawals (GIPS, CFA Institute);
- the percent of a daily index that would produce the same growth over the same days: the figure a fixed income contract states (`110% of CDI`).

## Goals

| ID | Goal |
|---|---|
| G1 | The report returns the time-weighted return of the period. |
| G2 | The report compares that return with a daily index: the index's own growth and the equivalent percent of the index. |
| G3 | The return screen lists savings and reserve accounts, so a savings account can be measured on its own. |
| G4 | Each figure means what its name says for the product measured: a rate a year and a percent of the index for a product that pays a rate, the period return beside the index for a product with a market price (R11–R13). |
| G5 | The report states the return net of inflation over whole months (R16). |

## Non-goals

| ID | Non-goal |
|---|---|
| N1 | A return net of taxes (income tax, IOF). Yield is gross (investments design NG3). |
| N2 | Inflation over part of a month. A real return covers whole months (R16). |
| N3 | Comparison with a monthly index. A monthly rate covers days outside a period that starts or ends inside the month. |
| N4 | Annualizing a return on a calendar basis, or without a daily index. A rate a year exists only on the year basis on which the index is quoted (R9). |
| N5 | A comparison on the trailing return of a goal (S130). |

## Rules

**R1 Time-weighted return.** The period `(from − 1, to]` splits at each date with an external flow (the flows of section 4.3, in `value_in`; for a tree under R19, the flows of R19). `V(d)` is the valued balance of the tree at the end of day `d`, the day's flows included. `F(d)` is the sum of the flows of day `d`. A subperiod ends at each flow date and at `to`; its return is `r = (V(end) − F(end) − V(start)) / V(start)`. A flow therefore earns from the next day, the convention of a CDI deposit. `period_bp` is `(Π(1 + r) − 1) × 10000`, rounded half to even. A subperiod with `V(start) = 0` and zero gain contributes 1. A subperiod with `V(start) ≤ 0` and a nonzero gain, or a factor `1 + r ≤ 0`, leaves `period_bp` absent, except that under R19 a subperiod with `V(start) = 0` contributes 1 whatever its gain, the gain being yield (R20).

**R2 Index.** The query parameter `index` names an enabled index with stored rates (`^[a-z0-9_]{2,40}$`). Without it, the index is the default of the book's region (`defaultBenchmark` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`): `cdi` for `BR`, none for any other region. A named index that is not enabled or has no rate is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`; a named index whose period is not `day` is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`. A default index that is not enabled or has no rate leaves `benchmark` absent.

**R3 Window.** For a tree outside R19, `benchmark.from` is `from` when the tree holds a value at the end of `from − 1`; otherwise it is the day after the first flow, since a flow earns from the next day. A tree that holds nothing and has no flow in the period leaves `benchmark` absent. `until` is the date of the last rate of the index in `[benchmark.from, to]`. Rates are dated on the business day they apply to, as in the accrual (section 4.2). With no rate in `[benchmark.from, to]`, `benchmark` is absent. `benchmark.return_bp` is R1 over `(from − 1, until]`; the days before `benchmark.from` contribute a factor of 1. A tree under R19 uses R14, R18 and R20 in place of this rule.

**R4 Index growth.** `benchmark.index_bp` is `(Π(1 + rate_d) − 1) × 10000` over the rates dated in `[benchmark.from, until]`, rounded half to even.

**R5 Equivalent percent.** `benchmark.percent_bp` is `p × 10000` for the `p` that solves `Π(1 + p × rate_d) = 1 + return` over the same rates, rounded half to even: 10000 is 100% of the index. The product grows with `p` when every rate is positive, so bisection over `p ∈ [−10, 10]` finds the one solution. `percent_bp` is absent when a rate is not positive, when `benchmark.return_bp` is absent, or when no `p` in the interval solves the equation. The solver uses floating point; the result leaves as an integer (AG2).

**R6 Contract.** `GET /reports/return` takes `index` and returns `period_bp` (integer, optional) and `benchmark` (optional: `index`, `from`, `until`, `index_bp`, and the optional `return_bp`, `percent_bp`, `annual_bp` and `index_annual_bp`). The tool view `return` of `get_portfolio` returns the same two fields with the default index; a tool schema has no null (ADR 012 T4), so an undefined value and a zero value are both absent there.

**R7 Screen.** The account list of the return screen adds the asset accounts of kind `savings` and `reserve` after the investment accounts, sorted by label. Under the annual rate, the screen shows the period return and, when `benchmark` is present, the index growth and the equivalent percent with the window `benchmark.from`–`until`.

**R8 Balance sheet.** On the Accounts screen, a group table that holds an account a return can measure (R7), whose parent is not one, adds the column "Yield". The cell states `benchmark.days` as "N d" and, on one line, `benchmark.annual_bp` followed by "a year" when `days` is 21 or more; with fewer days, or without `annual_bp`, the cell states `period_bp` followed by "in N d". The muted tone states `percent_bp` of the index, followed by "±X pp" when `percent_error_bp` is 100 or more (R22). Without `benchmark`, the cell shows `period_bp` and, when `unavailable_reason` is present, its text (R23). The window is the 90 days that end at the date of the sheet, the window of the row's balance trend, with the default index. Below 600 px the rate stands alone under the balance, on the line of the row's description, with its days. A checking account carries no yield: its income postings are mostly salary, and R1 counts income as return.

**R9 Rate a year.** `benchmark.annual_bp` is `((1 + return)^(B / n) − 1) × 10000` and `benchmark.index_annual_bp` is `((Π(1 + rate_d))^(B / n) − 1) × 10000`, both rounded half to even. `n` is the number of rates in `[benchmark.from, until]`, and `B` is the number of rate days in the year on which the index is quoted: 252 for `cdi` (`indexYearBasis` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`). An account paid 100% of the index has `annual_bp` equal to `index_annual_bp`. Both are absent when `B` is unknown; `annual_bp` is absent when `return_bp` is.

**R10 Account page.** The figures of an account that R8 measures add "Yield over 12 months": `annual_bp` over the 365 days that end today, and a note with `percent_bp` of the index, `index_annual_bp` and the window `benchmark.from`–`until`. Without `annual_bp`, the figure is absent.


**R11 Profile.** `profile` is `rate` when each asset account of the tree holds the `value_in` currency or a `fixed_income` instrument quoted in `value_in`, and `market` otherwise: a stock, an ETF, a REIT, a fund, a crypto asset, a depositary receipt or a foreign currency. A `market` return carries the price of an asset or an exchange rate; a `rate` return carries a rate.

**R12 Figures of a profile.** `annual_bp` (R9) and `percent_bp` (R5) exist only for `rate`. A `market` return is not annualized below a year, the rule of the GIPS standards (CFA Institute, 2020) for returns of periods shorter than a year, and a percent of an index states how much of a rate a product paid, which a market price does not. Both are absent for `market`; the screens state the period return beside the index.

**R13 Excess.** `benchmark.excess_bp` is `return_bp − index_bp`, the difference in percentage points between the return and the index over the same days, for both profiles. It is absent when `return_bp` is.

**R14 Days held.** A subperiod of R1 whose start value is 0 holds no money: the rates dated on its days, `(start, end]`, leave the comparison, so an account emptied and filled again is compared only over the days it held money. For a tree under R19 a rate dated `d` is compared when the tree holds money at the end of `d` (R20): the rate of the day of a deposit is compared, and the rate of the day of a withdrawal that empties the tree is not. `benchmark.days` is the number of rates compared; `benchmark.from` is the date of the first one. With no rate left, `benchmark` is absent. This replaces the first sentence of R3: an account opened inside the period is the case of a first subperiod that starts at 0.

**R15 Currency of the index.** An index belongs to a currency: `cdi` to `BRL` (`indexCurrency` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`). When `value_in` is another currency, the comparison is absent: a return in dollars does not compare with a rate paid in reais. An index without a known currency compares in any currency.

**R16 Inflation.** `inflation` states the return net of inflation. Its index is the region's default (`defaultInflation`: `ipca` for `BR`, none otherwise), a monthly index whose rate is dated on the first day of its month. Its months are the calendar months that lie whole inside the window of the comparison, `[since, to]`, and on every day of which the tree held money at the start of the day (R14). `since` is the first day the tree held money; for a tree under R19 the window is `(A, B]` of R18 and R20. The run starts at the first such month and stops at the first month that has no stored rate or has a day without money. `inflation.from` is the first day of the first month and `inflation.until` the last day of the last one. `inflation.index_bp` is `(Π(1 + rate_m) − 1) × 10000`; `inflation.return_bp` is R1 over `(inflation.from − 1, inflation.until]`; `inflation.real_bp` is `((1 + return) / (1 + inflation) − 1) × 10000` (Fisher). Each is rounded half to even. `inflation` is absent without a month, without an index for the region, or when the return of its months is undefined. It applies to both profiles; it is absent when `value_in` is not the currency of the index (R15: `ipca` belongs to `BRL`).

**R18 Recorded yield.** A rate tree that holds only the `value_in` currency, with no `fixed_income` instrument, is a tree under R19: it earns only what the ledger records against the Yield account. Its comparison covers `(A, B]`: B is the date of its last yield credit in `[from − 1, to]`; A is the date of its first yield credit in `[from − 1, to]`, moved by R20, the day before `from` included so a daily credit loses no day. The return is R1 over `(A, B]`, with the flows of R19; the inflation months are the whole months inside `(A, B]` (R16). The index rates compared are those of R20. With no yield credit in `[from − 1, to]`, `benchmark` and `inflation` are absent, reason `no_yield_recorded`; with A equal to B, reason `single_credit` (R23). `benchmark.from` is the date of the first rate compared and `benchmark.skipped` counts the rates of `[from, to]` left out. A rate tree that holds a `fixed_income` instrument is not covered: its yield is in the price, with no credit, and it uses R14 only. A balance synced from an institution that reports it net of the income tax it provisions records a yield net of that tax, below the gross rate of the contract; the screens say so.


**R19 Recorded yield of a cash tree.** In a rate tree that holds only the `value_in` currency (no `fixed_income` instrument), the result is the postings against the system account of role `yield` and its sub-accounts. Every other change of the balance of the tree is a flow: per transaction, the flow is the sum of the postings against the tree plus the sum of the postings against the yield accounts, so a transfer, a purchase, any other income or expense, a tax, a fee, an equity adjustment and an opening balance are flows, and a transaction internal to the tree or a pure yield credit nets 0. The yield is gross (N1). A and B of R18 use yield postings only. The report returns `excluded`: the income and expense amounts of `[from, to]` that were treated as flows, each with up to 3 category names, the largest first. A tree that holds a security keeps section 4.3 and R14.

**R20 Credit dates.** A credit observed on day C holds the rate of the days before C and cannot hold the rate of C, which is published after C ends. The rates compared are therefore those dated in `[A, B)`, on days the tree holds money at the end of the day (R14); the return is R1 over `(A, B]`. With A equal to B, `benchmark` is absent, reason `single_credit`. A flow dated strictly inside a gap of more than 7 calendar days between two consecutive credits moves A to the credit that ends the latest such gap, and `benchmark.restarted` is true: the credit after the flow pays on a balance the index days before it did not hold. `benchmark.credits` is the number of yield credits in `(A, B]`. Test builders credit the rate of a day on the next CDI day.

**R21 Yield column.** The header of the column of R8 is "Yield". Each cell, and the phone row, states `benchmark.days` as "N d". `annual_bp` is shown only when `days` is 21 or more; with fewer days the cell shows the period return "in N d" and `percent_bp`. The "since" label and the 7-day rule of the former R8 are removed.

**R22 Error of the percent.** `benchmark.percent_error_bp` is `ceil(5000 × n / Y)` for a tree under R19, with `n` the number of credits in `(A, B]` and `Y` their sum in minor units: each credit is rounded to half a minor unit, so the figures `percent_bp` and `annual_bp` are off by at most that fraction of themselves. Screens append "±X pp", X being `percent_bp` times the error, in points, when the error is 100 or more. `percent_bp` and `annual_bp` are absent when the error exceeds 500 or `Y` is not positive, reason `too_small`; `percent_error_bp` is absent when `Y` is not positive.

**R23 Reasons.** `unavailable_reason` (`^[a-z_]{2,40}$`) states why `benchmark`, or its `percent_bp` and `annual_bp`, is absent: `no_index_for_region` (no default index for the region, or one that is not enabled or has no rate), `currency_mismatch` (R15), `nothing_held`, `no_yield_recorded` (R18), `single_credit` (R20) and `too_small` (R22). The first reason found stays. The tool view carries the same field. Portuguese texts put no article before the index name ("índice {index}").
**R17 Screens.** Every figure the report holds is shown, named for what it is, with the days it covers. A negative return is written in the danger tone.

| Place | `rate` | `market` |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts, column "Yield" (R8, R21) | `annual_bp` a year (from 21 days) · `percent_bp` of the index | the period return · the index over the same days |
| Accounts, phone row | `annual_bp` a year (from 21 days), with `days` | the period return, with `days` |
| Account page, section "Yield over 12 months" | card "Rate a year" (`annual_bp`; note: the period return, `days` and the window); card "Percent of the index" (`percent_bp`, with "±X pp" from `percent_error_bp` 100; note: the index a year, `index_annual_bp`); card "Real return" (`real_bp`; note: the inflation and its months); when `benchmark` is absent, the inflation group alone with the text of `unavailable_reason` | card "Return" (`return_bp`; note: `days` and the window); card "Against the index" (`excess_bp` in percentage points; note: the index over the same days); card "Real return" |
| Portfolio, return section | PC10–PC13 of `docs/specs/2026-11-07-portfolio-clarity-design.md` | the same |

R8 and R10 hold where R17 does not replace them.
