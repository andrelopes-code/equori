# Return compared with an index

Amends `docs/specs/2026-10-23-investments-design.md` (NG5, section 4.3). Amendment A208.

## Intent

The return report (`GET /reports/return`) gives the money-weighted annual return of an account tree. It does not state whether a savings account sold as "100% of CDI" paid 100% of CDI. This design adds two figures to the report:

- the time-weighted return of the period: the growth of one unit of money held in the tree for the whole period, independent of the size and timing of deposits and withdrawals (GIPS, CFA Institute);
- the percent of a daily index that would produce the same growth over the same days: the figure a fixed income contract states (`110% of CDI`).

## Goals

| ID | Goal |
|---|---|
| G1 | The report returns the time-weighted return of the period. |
| G2 | The report compares that return with a daily index: the index's own growth and the equivalent percent of the index. |
| G3 | The return screen lists savings and reserve accounts, so a savings account can be measured on its own. |
| G4 | Each figure means what its name says for the product measured: a rate a year and a percent of the index for a product that pays a rate, the period return beside the index for a product with a market price (R11–R13). |
| G5 | The report states the return net of inflation over whole months (R16). |

## Non-goals

| ID | Non-goal |
|---|---|
| N1 | A return net of taxes (income tax, IOF). Yield is gross (investments design NG3). |
| N2 | Inflation over part of a month. A real return covers whole months (R16). |
| N3 | Comparison with a monthly index. A monthly rate covers days outside a period that starts or ends inside the month. |
| N4 | Annualizing a return on a calendar basis, or without a daily index. A rate a year exists only on the year basis on which the index is quoted (R9). |
| N5 | A comparison on the trailing return of a goal (S130). |

## Rules

**R1 Time-weighted return.** The period `(from − 1, to]` splits at each date with an external flow (the flows of section 4.3, in `value_in`; for a tree under R19, the flows of R19). `V(d)` is the valued balance of the tree at the end of day `d`, the day's flows included. `F(d)` is the sum of the flows of day `d`. A subperiod ends at each flow date and at `to`; its return is `r = (V(end) − F(end) − V(start)) / V(start)`. A flow therefore earns from the next day, the convention of a CDI deposit. `period_bp` is `(Π(1 + r) − 1) × 10000`, rounded half to even. A subperiod with `V(start) = 0` and zero gain contributes 1. A subperiod with `V(start) ≤ 0` and a nonzero gain, or a factor `1 + r ≤ 0`, leaves `period_bp` absent, except that under R19 a subperiod with `V(start) = 0` contributes 1 whatever its gain, the gain being yield (R20).

**R2 Index.** The query parameter `index` names an enabled index with stored rates (`^[a-z0-9_]{2,40}$`). Without it, the index is the default of the book's region (`defaultBenchmark` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`): `cdi` for `BR`, none for any other region. A named index that is not enabled or has no rate is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`; a named index whose period is not `day` is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`. A default index that is not enabled or has no rate leaves `benchmark` absent.

**R3 Window.** For a tree outside R19, `benchmark.from` is `from` when the tree holds a value at the end of `from − 1`; otherwise it is the day after the first flow, since a flow earns from the next day. A tree that holds nothing and has no flow in the period leaves `benchmark` absent. `until` is the date of the last rate of the index in `[benchmark.from, to]`. Rates are dated on the business day they apply to, as in the accrual (section 4.2). With no rate in `[benchmark.from, to]`, `benchmark` is absent. `benchmark.return_bp` is R1 over `(from − 1, until]`; the days before `benchmark.from` contribute a factor of 1. A tree under R19 uses R14, R18 and R20 in place of this rule.

**R4 Index growth.** `benchmark.index_bp` is `(Π(1 + rate_d) − 1) × 10000` over the rates dated in `[benchmark.from, until]`, rounded half to even.

**R5 Equivalent percent.** `benchmark.percent_bp` is `p × 10000` for the `p` that solves `Π(1 + p × rate_d) = 1 + return` over the same rates, rounded half to even: 10000 is 100% of the index. The product grows with `p` when every rate is positive, so bisection over `p ∈ [−10, 10]` finds the one solution. `percent_bp` is absent when a rate is not positive, when `benchmark.return_bp` is absent, or when no `p` in the interval solves the equation. The solver uses floating point; the result leaves as an integer (AG2).

**R6 Contract.** `GET /reports/return` takes `index` and returns `period_bp` (integer, optional) and `benchmark` (optional: `index`, `from`, `until`, `index_bp`, `started_late` (boolean, always present), and the optional `return_bp`, `percent_bp`, `annual_bp` and `index_annual_bp`). The tool view `return` of `get_portfolio` returns the same two fields with the default index; a tool schema has no null (ADR 012 T4), so an undefined value and a zero value are both absent there.

**R7 Screen.** The account list of the return screen adds the asset accounts of kind `savings` and `reserve` after the investment accounts, sorted by label. Under the annual rate, the screen shows the period return and, when `benchmark` is present, the index growth and the equivalent percent with the window `benchmark.from`–`until`.

**R8 Balance sheet.** On the Accounts screen, a group table that holds an account a return can measure (R7), whose parent is not one, adds the column "Yield". The cell is two fixed lines, right-aligned, each without wrapping (R21). The cell states, on the first line, `benchmark.annual_bp` followed by "a year", for any `days`; without `annual_bp`, the cell states `benchmark.return_bp`, or `period_bp` without `return_bp`. The muted tone states `percent_bp` of the index, followed by "±X pp" when `percent_error_bp` is 100 or more (R22). The cell states no number of days (R21). Without `benchmark`, the cell shows `period_bp` followed by "since {date}", the first day of the window, beside the text of `unavailable_reason` (R23), for the reasons `no_index_for_region`, `currency_mismatch`, `no_rate_in_window` and `percent_undefined`. For `no_yield_recorded`, `single_credit`, `nothing_held` and any reason without a text in R23, the cell shows the text alone: the return of a period with no recorded yield reads as zero and misleads. The window is the 90 days that end at the date of the sheet, the window of the row's balance trend, with the default index. Below 600 px the rate stands alone under the balance, on the line of the row's description, with its "since {date}" when R21 gives one. A checking account carries no yield: its income postings are mostly salary, and R1 counts income as return.

**R9 Rate a year.** `benchmark.annual_bp` is `((1 + return)^(B / n) − 1) × 10000` and `benchmark.index_annual_bp` is `((Π(1 + rate_d))^(B / n) − 1) × 10000`, both rounded half to even. `n` is the number of rates in `[benchmark.from, until]`, and `B` is the number of rate days in the year on which the index is quoted: 252 for `cdi` (`indexYearBasis` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`). An account paid 100% of the index has `annual_bp` equal to `index_annual_bp`. Both are absent when `B` is unknown; `annual_bp` is absent when `return_bp` is.

**R10 Account page.** The figures of an account that R8 measures add "Yield over 12 months": `annual_bp` over the 365 days that end today, and a note with `percent_bp` of the index, `index_annual_bp` and the window `benchmark.from`–`until`. Without `annual_bp`, the figure is absent.


**R11 Profile.** `profile` is `rate` when each asset account of the tree holds the `value_in` currency or a `fixed_income` instrument quoted in `value_in`, and `market` otherwise: a stock, an ETF, a REIT, a fund, a crypto asset, a depositary receipt or a foreign currency. A `market` return carries the price of an asset or an exchange rate; a `rate` return carries a rate.

**R12 Figures of a profile.** `annual_bp` (R9) and `percent_bp` (R5) exist only for `rate`. A `market` return is not annualized below a year, the rule of the GIPS standards (CFA Institute, 2020) for returns of periods shorter than a year, and a percent of an index states how much of a rate a product paid, which a market price does not. Both are absent for `market`; the screens state the period return beside the index.

**R13 Excess.** `benchmark.excess_bp` is `return_bp − index_bp`, the difference in percentage points between the return and the index over the same days, for both profiles. It is absent when `return_bp` is.

**R14 Days held.** A subperiod of R1 whose start value is 0 holds no money: the rates dated on its days, `(start, end]`, leave the comparison, so an account emptied and filled again is compared only over the days it held money. For a tree under R19 a rate dated `d` is compared when the tree holds money at the end of `d` (R20): the rate of the day of a deposit is compared, and the rate of the day of a withdrawal that empties the tree is not. `benchmark.days` is the number of rates compared; `benchmark.from` is the date of the first one. With no rate left, `benchmark` is absent. This replaces the first sentence of R3: an account opened inside the period is the case of a first subperiod that starts at 0.

**R15 Currency of the index.** An index belongs to a currency: `cdi` to `BRL` (`indexCurrency` in `internal/portfolio/region_defaults.go`). When `value_in` is another currency, the comparison is absent: a return in dollars does not compare with a rate paid in reais. An index without a known currency compares in any currency.

**R16 Inflation.** `inflation` states the return net of inflation. Its index is the region's default (`defaultInflation`: `ipca` for `BR`, none otherwise), a monthly index whose rate is dated on the first day of its month. Its months are the calendar months that lie whole inside the window of the comparison, `[since, to]`, and on every day of which the tree held money at the start of the day (R14). `since` is the first day the tree held money; for a tree under R19 the window is `(A, B]` of R18 and R20. The run starts at the first such month and stops at the first month that has no stored rate or has a day without money. `inflation.from` is the first day of the first month and `inflation.until` the last day of the last one. `inflation.index_bp` is `(Π(1 + rate_m) − 1) × 10000`; `inflation.return_bp` is R1 over `(inflation.from − 1, inflation.until]`; `inflation.real_bp` is `((1 + return) / (1 + inflation) − 1) × 10000` (Fisher). Each is rounded half to even. `inflation` is absent without a month, without an index for the region, or when the return of its months is undefined. It applies to both profiles; it is absent when `value_in` is not the currency of the index (R15: `ipca` belongs to `BRL`).

**R18 Recorded yield.** A rate tree that holds only the `value_in` currency, with no `fixed_income` instrument, is a tree under R19: it earns only what the ledger records against the Yield account. Its comparison covers `(A, B]`: B is the date of its last yield credit in `[from − 1, to]` dated on or before the day after the last stored rate of the index in that range (the conservative limit of R20); A is the date of its first yield credit in `[from − 1, to]`, moved by R20, the day before `from` included so a daily credit loses no day. The return is R1 over `(A, B]`, with the flows of R19; the inflation months are the whole months inside `(A, B]` (R16). The index rates compared are those of R20. With no yield credit in `[from − 1, to]`, `benchmark` and `inflation` are absent, reason `no_yield_recorded`; with A equal to B, reason `single_credit` (R23). `benchmark.from` is the date of the first rate compared and `benchmark.skipped` counts the rates of `[from, to]` left out. A rate tree that holds a `fixed_income` instrument is not covered: its yield is in the price, with no credit, and it uses R14 only. A balance synced from an institution that reports it net of the income tax it provisions records a yield net of that tax, below the gross rate of the contract; the screens say so.


**R19 Recorded yield of a cash tree.** In a rate tree that holds only the `value_in` currency (no `fixed_income` instrument), the result is the postings against the system account of role `yield` and its sub-accounts. Every other change of the balance of the tree is a flow. Per transaction, `Y` is the negated sum of the postings against the yield accounts, as a weight in the balancing currency. `Y` is allocated to the balance accounts that receive it, in proportion to what each receives. For `Y > 0` the receivers are the positive postings of asset and liability accounts in the transaction: `T⁺` is the sum of those against the tree and `O⁺` the sum of those against other accounts, and the credit is `Y × T⁺ / (T⁺ + O⁺)`. For `Y < 0` (a reversal) the same holds with the negative postings. Without a receiver the credit is 0. The credit is the same share of the native amount the tree receives, and the flow is the native amount of the tree less the credit, so a flow is negative when the credit exceeds what the tree receives. So a transfer, a purchase, any other income or expense, a tax, a fee, an equity adjustment and an opening balance are flows, the yield booked to an account outside the tree is not a credit of the tree, and a transaction internal to the tree or a pure yield credit leaves no flow. A yield of 100.00 against a savings account that receives 85.00 and a Taxes expense of 15.00 is a credit of 100.00 and a flow of −15.00: the credit is gross and the tax is an outflow. A negative credit (a reversal of yield) reduces the yield credited but is never a credit date. A flow dated after the first rate of the index and before its last, on a day without a rate (a weekend or a holiday), moves to the next day with a rate: a bank pays a deposit from its first business day, and the credit of that day pays the rate of the day before on the balance of the day before. The yield is gross (N1). A and B of R18 use positive credits only. The report returns `excluded`: the income and expense postings of `[from, to]`, a range that can be wider than the compared window `(A, B]`, of the transactions that leave a flow under this rule, each with up to 3 category names, the largest first. A tree that holds a security keeps section 4.3 and R14.

**R20 Credit dates.** A credit observed on day C holds the rate of the days before C and cannot hold the rate of C, which is published after C ends. The rates compared are therefore those dated in `[A, B)`, on days the tree holds money at the end of the day (R14); the return is R1 over `(A, B]`. With A equal to B, `benchmark` is absent, reason `single_credit`. Credits dated after the day that follows the last stored rate of the index in `[from − 1, to]` leave the window, since the rate of a day is published after it: B is the last credit on or before that day. The limit is conservative on purpose: a credit dated after a weekend or holiday that pays the last stored rate lies beyond it and is left out, because business days are regional and no calendar is assumed. The figure loses at most those credits and never counts a rate that is not stored. With no credit after A within it, `benchmark` is absent, reason `no_rate_in_window`. A flow dated strictly inside a gap of more than 7 calendar days between two consecutive credits moves A to the credit that ends the latest such gap, and `benchmark.restarted` is true: the credit after the flow pays on a balance the index days before it did not hold. `benchmark.credits` is the number of positive yield credits in `(A, B]`. Test builders credit the rate of a day on the next CDI day.

**R21 Yield column.** The cell has two lines, right-aligned, each `nowrap`: line 1 is the primary figure ("{annual} a year" for a rate profile with `annual_bp`, the return otherwise); line 2, muted, joins the secondary parts with " · " (percent of the index, "{index} {indexReturn}" or the text of the reason, then "since {date}"). Only the text of a reason may wrap, on line 2. The header of the column of R8 is "Yield". Each cell, and the phone row, states no number of days: "N d" does not read as a count of rate days. A rate profile with `annual_bp` reads "{annual} a year · {percent} of {index}" (Portuguese "{annual} a.a. · {percent} do índice {index}"), whatever `days` is; the guard against noise is R22 alone. `benchmark.started_late` is true when at least one index rate dated in `[from, to]` before `benchmark.from` was left out: the comparison starts after the first rate day of the window, by R14 (days without money) or by R18 and R20 (yield recorded later); it is false otherwise. The tool view omits it when false. When `started_late` is true, " · since {date}" follows (Portuguese "desde {date}"), the date being `benchmark.from` in the short numeric form of the locale (dd/mm in pt-BR, MM/dd in en). A market profile, or a rate profile without `annual_bp`, reads "{return} since {date} · {index} {indexReturn}" when `started_late` is true and "{return} · {index} {indexReturn}" otherwise. The phone row keeps the figure and the "since" date and omits the percent. The account page keeps its period line with the dates and the days spelled out (R10, R17).

**R22 Error of the percent.** `benchmark.percent_error_bp` is `ceil(5000 × n / Y)` for a tree under R19, with `n` the number of tree postings of the positive credits in `(A, B]` and `Y` the sum of the credits of `(A, B]`, reversals included, in minor units: each tree posting of a credit is rounded to half a minor unit, so the figures `percent_bp` and `annual_bp` are off by at most that fraction of themselves. The bound applies to `percent_bp`, and `annual_bp` is shown only when `percent_bp` is. Screens append "±X pp", X being `percent_bp` times the error, in points, when the error is 100 or more. `percent_bp` and `annual_bp` are absent when the error exceeds 500 or `Y` is not positive, reason `too_small`; `percent_error_bp` is absent when `Y` is not positive. For the rate profile, `percent_bp` and `annual_bp` are also absent when `benchmark.days` is below 5, reason `too_few_days`: with few compared days the timing of one credit dominates, and one day of mismatch moves the percent by 1/`days`. `return_bp`, `index_bp`, the window and the period return stay. A tree whose percent is already absent for another reason keeps that reason (R23).

**R23 Reasons.** `unavailable_reason` (`^[a-z_]{2,40}$`) states why `benchmark`, or its `percent_bp` and `annual_bp`, is absent: `no_index_for_region` (no default index for the region, or one that is not enabled or has no rate), `currency_mismatch` (R15), `nothing_held`, `no_yield_recorded` (R18), `single_credit` (R20), `too_small` (R22), `too_few_days` (R22: fewer than 5 compared days) and `no_rate_in_window` (the index has no rate in the compared window, or no credit after A lies within its rates) and `percent_undefined` (`return_bp` is absent, or `percent_bp` is absent because a rate is not positive or no `p` solves R5). The first reason found stays. The tool view carries the same field. Portuguese texts put no article before the index name ("índice {index}").
**R17 Screens.** Every figure the report holds is shown, named for what it is; the account page states the days it covers, the column of R8 states the day a late start begins from (R21). A negative return is written in the danger tone.

## Scenarios

- **S302** A savings account of 10,000,000.00 whose credits are, on each CDI day after the first, the CDI of the days since the previous credit on its balance, over the eleven CDI days 2026-01-02 to 2026-01-16: `benchmark` has `index` `cdi`, `from` 2026-01-05, `until` 2026-01-15, `days` 9, `skipped` 2, `credits` 9, `index_bp` 50, `return_bp` 50, `percent_bp` 10000, `annual_bp` 1490, `index_annual_bp` 1490 and `percent_error_bp` 1; `period_bp` 55. The credit of 2026-01-05 is A and the credit of 2026-01-16 is B: the rates of 2026-01-05 to 2026-01-15 are compared.
- **S303** The same account receives a deposit of 10,000,000.00 from checking on 2026-01-06: the figures of S302 hold. With `to` 2026-01-20, after the last stored rate, `until` stays 2026-01-15.
- **S304** `index=ipca` (a monthly index) is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`; `index=sofr` with no enabled source is 400 `invalid_request` on `index`. Both are 400 also when the tree held no money in the period.
- **S305** A savings account that receives its first deposit of 10,000,000.00 on 2026-01-05 and then the credits of S302, measured from 2026-01-02 to 2026-01-16: the first credit is on 2026-01-06, so `benchmark.from` is 2026-01-06, `until` 2026-01-15, `days` 8, `skipped` 3, `credits` 8, `index_bp` 44 and `percent_bp` 10000; `period_bp` 50.
- **S306** A brokerage that holds 10 PETR4 bought at 30.00 and priced at 27.00 at the end of the period: `profile` `market`; `benchmark` has `return_bp` −1000, `excess_bp` −1000 − `index_bp`, and no `annual_bp` or `percent_bp`.
- **S307** The savings account of S302, emptied by a withdrawal to checking on 2026-01-06 and filled again on 2026-01-07: the tree holds no money at the end of 2026-01-06, so that rate leaves the comparison; `benchmark.from` is 2026-01-05, `until` 2026-01-15, `days` 8, `skipped` 3, `credits` 8, `index_bp` 44 and `percent_bp` 10000; `period_bp` 50.
- **S308** The savings account of S302 opened on 2025-10-01 with a yield of 1.00 recorded on 2025-10-31 against the Yield account, with IPCA 0.50% for 2025-11 and 0.40% for 2025-12, measured from 2025-11-01 to 2026-01-09: `inflation` has `index` `ipca`, `from` 2025-11-01, `until` 2025-12-31 and `index_bp` 90, `return_bp` 0 (the CDI is credited only in January) and `real_bp` -89. The yield of 2025-10-31 is A, the day before `from`, and the credit of 2026-01-09 is B: both months lie inside (A, B].
- **S309** A book of region `US` gets no `benchmark` and no `inflation`, reason `no_index_for_region`; `period_bp` is present. A book of region `BR` that values the report in `USD` gets neither, reason `currency_mismatch`.
- **S310** The savings account of S302 whose credits of 2026-01-05 and 2026-01-06 are not recorded: `benchmark.from` 2026-01-07, `until` 2026-01-15, `days` 7, `skipped` 4, `credits` 7, `index_bp` 39 and `percent_bp` 10000; `period_bp` 44. With no credit recorded, `benchmark` is absent, reason `no_yield_recorded`, and `period_bp` is 0.
- **S311** A savings account of 8,135.94 held since 2025-12-01 whose credits are 99.0% of the CDI on 2026-01-05 and 2026-01-06, none on 2026-01-07 and 92.0% on each CDI day from 2026-01-08 to 2026-01-16, with a deposit of 1,000.00 on 2026-01-08 after the credit, measured from 2025-12-01 to 2026-01-16: `benchmark.from` 2026-01-05 (A is the credit of 2026-01-05), `until` 2026-01-15, `days` 9, `skipped` 2, `credits` 8, `index_bp` 50, `return_bp` 41, `percent_bp` 8255, `annual_bp` 1215, `index_annual_bp` 1490 and `percent_error_bp` 11; `period_bp` 47; `inflation` is absent, as no whole month lies in `(A, B]`.
- **S312** A brokerage that buys 10 PETR4 at 30.00 on 2025-11-15 (300.00 from checking) and prices it at 30.00 on 2025-11-15 and 2025-12-31, measured from 2025-11-01 to 2026-01-09: `inflation` has `from` 2025-12-01, `until` 2025-12-31, `index_bp` 40, `return_bp` 0 and `real_bp` -40; The tree first held money on 2025-11-15, so November is not a whole month of the window.
